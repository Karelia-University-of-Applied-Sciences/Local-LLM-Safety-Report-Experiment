Based on the report, two root causes were identified as contributing to the manufacturing safety incident:

1. **Task risk assessment did not adequately address the specific hazards of manually transporting large cladding sheets via internal staircases**

This root cause contributed to the incident in several ways:

* The task risk assessment failed to identify the inherent risks associated with manual transportation of large and heavy panels, such as losing control of the sheet or slipping on stairs.
* As a result, no alternative solutions were considered, such as using mechanical handling aids or finding an alternative route for transporting the sheets.
* This lack of consideration led to the decision to continue with manual transport via internal staircases, which ultimately resulted in the incident.

2. **No mechanical handling aid or alternative route was identified despite the size and weight of the panels making manual staircase transport inherently high risk**

This root cause contributed to the incident by:

* Failing to address the obvious risks associated with manually transporting large and heavy cladding sheets via internal staircases.
* Not considering alternative solutions, such as using mechanical handling aids (e.g., dollies or forklifts) that could have reduced the risks of manual transport.
* Leaving workers vulnerable to accidents, as evidenced by the incident where an operative lost control of a cladding sheet and struck a colleague.

These two root causes are interconnected, as the failure to adequately assess task risks led to the decision not to use mechanical handling aids or alternative routes. By identifying these root causes, the company can take corrective actions to prevent similar incidents in the future.
