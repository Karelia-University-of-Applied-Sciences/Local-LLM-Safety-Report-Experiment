Based on the report provided (Report ID: INC-2025-014), the root causes of the manufacturing safety incident where an electrical maintenance technician received a mild electric shock can be identified and explained as follows:

1. **Outdated Panel Wiring Diagram**: The panel wiring diagram did not reflect the addition of a secondary 24V DC control circuit, which was added two years prior to the incident. This contributed significantly because without accurate documentation, the technicians were unaware that additional live circuits could still be present even when certain parts were de-energised and locked out.

2. **Lockout/Tagout Procedure Insufficiency**: The existing lockout/tagout procedure did not mandate a verification test for live circuits after applying locks to ensure all energy sources are truly isolated. This oversight meant that any residual live circuits, such as the secondary 24V DC control circuit in this case, were not detected during the safety checks.

3. **Lack of Labeling on Secondary Power Supply Unit**: The report indicates that the exterior of the panel did not label the presence of a secondary power supply unit. This lack of clear labeling contributed to the incident as it would have been more apparent if there was proper identification, alerting technicians to check for and verify such secondary circuits during their lockout/tagout procedures.

These root causes collectively led to the technician being exposed to an energized circuit that they believed had been safely de-energised. The outdated wiring diagram provided inaccurate information about the system's current state, while the insufficient lockout/tagout procedure failed to ensure all possible live circuits were accounted for and verified as safe before maintenance began. Lastly, without clear labeling of secondary power sources on the panel, there was no immediate visual reminder or prompt for additional checks beyond what would normally be expected for a primary circuit isolation process.
