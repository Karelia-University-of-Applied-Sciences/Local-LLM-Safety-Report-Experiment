Based on the report provided (Report ID: INC-2025-028), two root causes of the manufacturing safety incident can be identified:

1. **Lack of Awareness about Mandatory Conveyor Stop Before Clearing Jams**: The operative was not fully aware that it is mandatory to stop the conveyor before attempting to clear a jam. This lack of awareness led to the decision to intervene directly with the moving part, which resulted in the laceration. Understanding and adhering to the correct safety procedures are critical for preventing accidents involving machinery where direct contact can lead to injury.

2. **Insufficient Accessibility to Emergency Stop Buttons**: The emergency stop buttons at Section 3 were positioned only at the ends of the conveyor section, approximately 3 metres away from where the jam occurred. This means that when a jam happened in an intermediate position along the conveyor, reaching the emergency stop button would have required the operative to move away from the jam site, potentially increasing the time needed to halt the belt and exposing the operative to greater risk during this maneuver.

These root causes contributed to the incident by creating a situation where safety protocols were not followed due to ignorance of procedures (awareness issue) and physical constraints that made adherence to those protocols difficult in practice (accessibility issue). The corrective actions aim to address both issues through better training, increased accessibility of emergency stop buttons, updating procedural instructions, and ensuring these are clearly communicated and displayed at critical points.
