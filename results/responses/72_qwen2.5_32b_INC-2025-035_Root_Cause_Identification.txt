Based on the report (Report ID: INC-2025-035), the root causes of the manufacturing safety incident can be identified as follows:

1. **No Replacement Date Marked on the Grinding Wheel**: The grinding wheel was in service beyond its recommended replacement interval, which is a critical factor leading to its failure. Not marking or recording the installation and planned replacement dates directly contributed to this oversight.

2. **Lack of Installation Record Maintenance**: Without an official record of when the grinding wheel was installed, there was no systematic way for staff to be aware of when it needed to be replaced based on its recommended service life.

3. **Pre-Use Inspection Checklist Omission**: The inspection checklist used before operating the bench grinder did not include a requirement to visually inspect the grinding wheel's surface for cracks or other signs of wear and tear, which would have alerted the technician to the compromised state of the wheel.

4. **Convenience Over Safety with Face Shields**: Full face shields were stored away from the grinding area, making it more convenient for technicians to use safety glasses instead. This convenience factor directly contributed to the decision not to use the full face shield as specified for the operation, which could have provided better protection against fragments ejected due to wheel failure.

These root causes collectively led to an unsafe condition where a worn-out and cracked grinding wheel was used without prior knowledge of its compromised state or necessary protective equipment, resulting in a near-miss incident. The corrective actions proposed aim at addressing these issues directly by enhancing record-keeping practices, updating safety protocols, improving the accessibility of full face shields, and reinforcing safety training among maintenance staff.
