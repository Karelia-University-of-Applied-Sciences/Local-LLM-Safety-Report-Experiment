Based on the report, I have identified two root causes that contributed to the manufacturing safety incident:

**1. Lack of Mandatory Pre-Use Inspection Checklist**

The report states that a mandatory pre-use inspection checklist did not exist for the cradle platform to verify materials were secured before operations commenced. This lack of standard procedure allowed the rubber packing components to become unsecured, leading to them falling from the cradle platform.

Explanation: Without a routine inspection process in place, it is likely that crew members did not regularly check the security of materials on the cradle platform, allowing the incident to occur. The absence of a pre-use checklist created an opportunity for human error and oversight, which contributed to the incident.

**2. Crew Members' Lack of Awareness of Hazards Associated with Unsecured Materials**

The report also notes that crew members lacked sufficient awareness of hazards associated with unsecured materials on cradle platforms operating at height above public access areas. This lack of knowledge or training allowed them to overlook the risks and did not prompt them to take necessary precautions.

Explanation: Crew members' inadequate understanding of the potential consequences of dropped objects contributed to the incident. If they had been better trained on the hazards associated with unsecured materials, they may have taken extra precautions to secure the rubber packing components before operations commenced, preventing the accident from occurring.
