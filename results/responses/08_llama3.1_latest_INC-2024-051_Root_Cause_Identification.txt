The root causes of the manufacturing safety incident described in the report are:

1. **Inadequate Personal Protective Equipment (PPE) availability**: The required chemical-resistant extended-cuff gloves were not available at the workstation because the stock had been depleted and not replenished.

Explanation: This lack of essential PPE led to the technician using standard nitrile gloves, which proved insufficient for handling the industrial solvent. As a result, the technician was exposed to skin irritation when the solvent splashed onto their forearms above the glove line.

2. **Inadequate PPE inventory management**: There was no minimum stock level alert system in place for PPE at chemical handling workstations.

Explanation: The absence of an alert system meant that the depletion of chemical-resistant gloves went unnoticed, allowing the technician to proceed with the task using inadequate PPE.

3. **Incorrect use of available PPE**: The technician chose to continue working with standard nitrile gloves rather than stopping work due to their unavailability.

Explanation: This decision likely stemmed from a lack of understanding or training on stop-work authority and PPE selection, which is mentioned as an area for refresher training in the corrective actions section. By continuing with inadequate PPE, the technician put themselves at risk of injury.

4. **Insufficient signage and communication**: The PPE requirements signage at chemical storage workstations was not up-to-date or clear.

Explanation: While this root cause is more indirect, it may have contributed to the technician's decision to continue working without proper PPE. If the signage had been clear and up-to-date, the technician may have been aware of the specific PPE requirements for the task at hand.
