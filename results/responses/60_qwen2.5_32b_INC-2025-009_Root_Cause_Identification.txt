Based on the report INC-2025-009, several root causes have been identified that contributed to the manufacturing safety incident involving a spray painter experiencing symptoms of mild dizziness and headache:

1. **Failure of One Extract Ventilation Fan**: The failure of one out of two extract ventilation fans in Spray Booth 2 significantly reduced its air extraction capacity by about half. This reduction allowed for a higher concentration of paint vapors, which contributed to the painter's discomfort despite wearing appropriate protective equipment.

2. **Insufficient Pre-Use Check Procedures**: The pre-use check for the spray booth relied solely on operator observation without quantitative measurement methods. As such, the reduced extraction capacity due to the fan failure was not detected before starting work. This indicates a flaw in the safety checks that could have prevented the incident if more rigorous and precise monitoring were employed.

3. **Overdue Preventive Maintenance**: The maintenance schedule for the ventilation fans was three weeks overdue. Regular preventive maintenance is crucial in identifying and addressing potential failures or inefficiencies in critical systems like ventilation, which are essential for worker safety in environments with high vapor concentrations.

Each of these root causes individually played a role in compromising the safe working conditions within Spray Booth 2, leading to the incident where the painter experienced mild symptoms. The corrective actions aim at improving monitoring and maintenance procedures to prevent such incidents from recurring.
