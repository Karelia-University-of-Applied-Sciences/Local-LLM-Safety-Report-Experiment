The root causes of the manufacturing safety incident reported in INC-2024-047 can be identified as follows:

1. **Exceeded Filter Service Interval**: The inline filter had exceeded its recommended service interval by 123 hours (500-hour recommendation vs 623-hours actual). This prolonged operation beyond the intended service life resulted in a reduced filtration efficiency, allowing metal shavings to accumulate and partially block the hydraulic return line. The accumulation of these shavings led to an increase in backpressure within the system, causing the unexpected pressure spike.

2. **Partial Blockage in Hydraulic Return Line**: The partial blockage caused by accumulated metal shavings is directly linked to the exceeded service interval of the inline filter. This blockage prevented proper fluid return and resulted in elevated pressures beyond the standard operating threshold (340 bar vs 280 bar). Had this pressure spike been uncontrolled, it could have led to equipment failure or other hazardous outcomes.

These root causes contributed significantly to the incident by creating an environment where hydraulic system components were stressed beyond their normal operational limits. The exceeded service interval of the inline filter meant that it was no longer effectively removing contaminants from the fluid stream, leading directly to the partial blockage and associated pressure issues in the return line.

The corrective actions proposed address these root causes by ensuring filters are replaced at appropriate intervals, conducting baseline checks on hydraulic pressures during pre-shift equipment inspections, revising filter replacement schedules across all production lines for better maintenance practices, and enhancing operator awareness through hazard-focused training. These steps aim to prevent similar incidents by improving the reliability of the hydraulic system and increasing operational safety through proactive monitoring and timely maintenance.
