Based on the report, I have identified three root causes of the manufacturing safety incident:

**Root Cause 1: No replacement date marked on the grinding wheel**

The fact that no replacement date was marked on the grinding wheel suggests a lack of adherence to maintenance schedules and records management. The grinding wheel had been in service beyond its recommended replacement interval, which implies that it was not properly monitored or tracked. This led to the failure of the grinding wheel, causing the incident.

**Root Cause 2: No installation record maintained**

The absence of an installation record for the grinding wheel indicates a lack of documentation and tracking of maintenance activities. Without a record of when the wheel was installed, it is unlikely that anyone would know when it should be replaced, leading to its extended use beyond its recommended lifespan.

**Root Cause 3: Inadequate safety procedures and equipment availability**

The report mentions two aspects of inadequate safety procedures:

* The pre-use inspection checklist did not include a visual inspection for cracks on the grinding wheel's surface. This means that potential defects were not being identified before use, increasing the risk of an incident.
* Full face shields were stored away from the grinding area, making them less convenient to wear than safety glasses. While the technician was wearing safety glasses (albeit with a cracked lens), this may have contributed to the severity of the incident or encouraged complacency.

These root causes interacted and exacerbated each other: without proper records and maintenance schedules, the grinding wheel was not properly inspected, leading to its failure; meanwhile, inadequate safety procedures and equipment availability made it more likely that an incident would occur when a failure did happen.
