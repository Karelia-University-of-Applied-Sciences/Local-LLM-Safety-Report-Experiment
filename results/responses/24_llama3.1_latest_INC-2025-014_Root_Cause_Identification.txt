Here are the root causes of the manufacturing safety incident and an explanation of how each contributed to the incident:

**Root Cause 1: Outdated Panel Wiring Diagram**

The outdated wiring diagram did not reflect a secondary power supply that had been added two years prior. This lack of up-to-date information led the technician to be unaware of the secondary circuit, which remained energized even after the main isolator was locked out.

**Contribution:** The outdated diagram created a false sense of security for the technician, who believed the control panel was fully de-energized when it actually still had an active circuit. This lack of knowledge led to the technician making contact with the live terminal on the secondary circuit.

**Root Cause 2: Inadequate Lockout/Tagout Procedure**

The lockout/tagout procedure did not require a live circuit verification test after lockout application. This meant that the technician was not required to check for any remaining energized circuits before starting work, which led to the incident.

**Contribution:** The lack of verification testing created an opportunity for the technician to overlook the secondary power supply and make contact with the live terminal without realizing it was still energized.

**Root Cause 3: Unlabelled Secondary Power Supply Unit**

The secondary power supply unit was not labelled on the panel exterior. This made it difficult for the technician to identify and recognize the presence of a separate power source, which remained energized even after the main isolator was locked out.

**Contribution:** The lack of labelling created confusion for the technician and contributed to their failure to identify the secondary circuit before making contact with the live terminal.
