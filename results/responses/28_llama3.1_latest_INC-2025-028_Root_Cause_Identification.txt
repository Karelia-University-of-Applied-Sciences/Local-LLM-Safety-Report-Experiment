Based on the report, I have identified two root causes of the manufacturing safety incident:

**Root Cause 1: Operative not fully aware that stopping the conveyor was mandatory before clearing jams**

This root cause contributed to the incident in several ways:

* The operative did not understand the importance of stopping the conveyor before attempting to clear a jam, which led them to take on the task while it was still moving.
* This lack of understanding likely resulted from inadequate training or communication about the procedure for clearing product jams.

**Root Cause 2: Emergency stop buttons at Section 3 positioned only at the ends of the conveyor section approximately 3 metres from where the jam occurred**

This root cause contributed to the incident in several ways:

* The positioning of emergency stop buttons only at the ends of the conveyor section made it difficult for the operative to stop the conveyor quickly and safely, especially when a jam occurred near the middle of the section.
* This layout created an undue risk that the operative would need to take on the task of clearing a jam while the conveyor was still moving, as there were no convenient means of stopping it.

These two root causes are interconnected, as the second root cause (inadequate positioning of emergency stop buttons) likely exacerbated the first root cause (operative not fully aware of safety procedures), creating a situation where the operative felt compelled to take on the task without properly stopping the conveyor.
