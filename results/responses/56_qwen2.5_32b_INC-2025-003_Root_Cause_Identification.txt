The root causes identified in the report for the manufacturing safety incident involving a CNC milling machine are:

1. **Inadequate Operator Training**: The training provided to operators did not clearly distinguish between a tool change pause and a full spindle stop. This lack of clear information led the machinist to believe that it was safe to open the guard door during the deceleration phase of the spindle, which was still rotating at approximately 200 RPM.

    - **Explanation**: Since the operator training did not cover the critical distinction between these two states (tool change pause and full stop), the machinist underestimated the risk associated with opening the guard. This misunderstanding resulted in a situation where the machinist's hand came dangerously close to the rotating spindle, highlighting a significant gap in the safety knowledge required for operating CNC milling machines safely.

2. **Production Time Pressure**: The report indicates that there was time pressure due to a backlog, which contributed to rushed behavior by the machinist during the inspection process.

    - **Explanation**: The combination of production pressures and a potentially challenging work environment can cause operators to make decisions or take actions under stress without fully considering safety protocols. In this case, it appears that the desire to meet deadlines might have prompted the operator to inspect the machine guard without ensuring that all moving parts were completely stopped, thus increasing the risk of injury.

These root causes contributed significantly to the incident by creating an unsafe situation where a critical procedural step (ensuring the spindle is fully stationary) was bypassed due to a lack of proper training and under the influence of time pressure. To prevent similar incidents, corrective actions focus on enhancing operator training, modifying procedures to include additional safety checks, and addressing production scheduling issues that can lead to rushed work practices.
