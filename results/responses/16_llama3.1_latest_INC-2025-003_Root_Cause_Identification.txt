Based on the report, two root causes were identified:

1. **Operator training did not cover distinction between tool change pause and full spindle stop**

This root cause contributed to the incident because the machinist was unaware that the machine's tool change cycle paused the spindle deceleration phase at approximately 200 RPM. The machinist assumed the spindle had stopped, which led them to open the guard door while the spindle was still rotating. This lack of understanding and training put the machinist at risk of severe injury.

2. **Production time pressure from a backlog contributed to rushed behavior**

This root cause also contributed to the incident because the machinist felt pressured to complete their task quickly due to the production backlog. This time pressure led them to act hastily and ignore proper safety protocols, such as waiting for the spindle to fully stop before inspecting the workpiece. The machinist's rushed behavior put themselves at risk of severe injury when they came close to the decelerating spindle.

In both cases, the root causes were related to human factors and a lack of attention to detail. The report highlights the importance of proper training and management practices in preventing similar incidents from occurring in the future.
